# Note to the Paylane Head of Support

**Subject: Your support assistant: what it does, what it won't do, and what we could add next**

Hi,

The first version of your support assistant is ready for you to try. Here is a short summary.

## What it does
- **Checks who it is talking to first.** Before it talks about any account, the customer has to give the email on file and the last 4 digits of their card. It never shows a full card number or the email address.
- **Fixes the problem before refunding.** If a customer doesn't recognise a charge, it explains what the charge is. If a "charge" is really a temporary hold, such as a hotel deposit, it explains that the hold will drop off. If a "double charge" is a pending payment, it explains that the pending payment will disappear.
- **Explains that a refund cancels the purchase.** Before refunding, it tells the customer what happens next. A subscription is cancelled and access ends. An order is cancelled and won't be delivered. It refunds only after the customer says yes.
- **Refunds subscriptions fairly.** Cancelling on the day the customer was charged gives a full refund. After that, it refunds only the unused days. For example, cancelling a $59.99 monthly plan halfway through gives back $30.00.
- **Checks the delivery before refunding an order:**
  - *On its way:* no refund. It offers to wait.
  - *Lost, or not yet sent:* it refunds once the customer agrees.
  - *Marked as delivered:* it asks the customer what shows it didn't arrive, such as a claim with the delivery company or a neighbour who checked. With that, it refunds. Without it, the case goes to your team.
- **Refunds small charges on the spot.** The charge has to be $75 or less and less than 30 days old.
- **Hands everything else to your team** and tells the customer, "A specialist will follow up by email."

## What it won't do
- Refund charges over $75, or 30 days old or older. It doesn't do partial refunds on bigger charges either. These go to your team.
- Refund the same charge twice.
- Unfreeze a card. It can explain why a card was frozen, then it passes the case to your team.
- Handle fraud claims, legal threats or account closures. These always go to a person.
- Promise a callback time, a manager or anything else the policy doesn't allow, however upset the customer is.

## How it decides, for your sign-off
- Per customer, refunds made by the assistant add up to **no more than $75 in any 30 days**. This stops someone splitting a big charge into small refund requests.
- **Every** fraud claim goes to a person, whatever the amount.
- Charges that are still **pending** can be refunded. A charge from **exactly 30 days ago** counts as too old.
- After **3 wrong verification attempts**, the chat stops sharing information and passes the case to your team.
- Refunds for subscriptions are **prorated** as described above, and a refund always **cancels** the subscription or order.

## What we could add next to make it a reliable, trusted chatbot
1. **Your sign-off on the rules above**, or any changes you want.
2. **Follow-up wording.** If you have a standard reply time, the assistant can quote it instead of the general "a specialist will follow up by email".
3. **Your approved wording** for the common fixes: how to cancel a subscription, how long holds take to drop off, and what counts as proof for a missing parcel.
4. **A shadow trial with your team.** Two or three of your agents run it alongside their normal work and flag anything that feels off, in tone or in decisions.
5. **Ongoing review of real chats**, with new cases added to the test set whenever something goes wrong.
6. **Monitoring** of refunds, escalations and blocked attempts, so problems show up early.

In our latest test run it handled all 26 of our practice conversations correctly.
